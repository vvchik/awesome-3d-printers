# Awesome 3D Printers

A curated catalog of open-design and community-documented FDM printers to build, modify, or study. Each design has **one catalog row**. The printer name links to its project repository when available, otherwise to the creator's project page or a useful community reference. Maker and family labels help you find related designs without repeating rows.

**Photos:** 📷 marks a planned image. Images can be added after checking permission, source, and consistent sizing. **Resources:** STEP/CAD links point to source files or their containing folder when available; “Files” may contain printable models without STEP. Video-only experimental rows are for studying a design, not a ready build package. A dash means a useful direct resource link has not yet been confirmed. Build cost, difficulty, and project status change, so the short descriptions are starting points rather than rankings.

**Stars / likes:** GitHub badges show the current star count (subject to badge caching). Printables and Thingiverse badges show likes on the linked model pages, checked 2026-10-20; these snapshots do not update automatically. Counts describe the linked page, which can cover a CAD assembly or one variant rather than an entire printer family, and are not directly comparable across platforms. `—` means no count for the primary link.

**Release dates:** The date is for the latest *listed* public printer revision or file release, not the first design in its family or the repository’s most recent commit. The revision is named in the cell when known; dates link to creator release notes or dated publications. Month or year precision means the source gives no exact day. `—` means a release date for that revision could not be verified.

**Sizes:** All figures are millimetres. Build volume is printable X × Y × Z, not the physical bed size. Exterior size is the reported body or frame width × depth × height; it may exclude a spool, moving-bed clearance, cables, or enclosure extras. `?` means that axis was not confirmed, and `—` means no trustworthy figure was found. “Configurable” and “installation-dependent” mean the design has no single fixed build volume. Variant names keep their build and exterior measurements paired. `†` marks approximate or revision-dependent figures from the [MaybeCube](https://github.com/martinbudden/MaybeCube#comparisons) or [BabyCube](https://github.com/martinbudden/BabyCube#comparisons) comparison tables; check the current maker files before planning a space. MaybeCube's own overview and variant table differ for MC350, so the row uses its labelled variant table. For circular deltas, `Ø` is print diameter. For belt printers, an open-ended print length is not converted into a finite volume.

## Start here

A few different reasons to choose a first project; these point to the full catalogue rows below.

- **Flying-gantry CoreXY:** [Voron 2.4](#voron-2-4) combines an enclosed design with detailed documentation, a large community, and kit options.
- **Speed-focused build:** [VzBot Vz330](#vzbot) is a platform for speed benchmarking, with AWD variants.
- **Classic full-size CoreXY:** [Voron Trident](#voron-trident) has a great assembly manual; its fixed gantry and independently driven Z bed come with a large community and many kit options.
- **Cross-gantry build:** [Annex K3 / Gasherbrum](#annex-k3) is a compact, rigid, speed-oriented design with a builder community.
- **Mostly printed CoreXY:** [Rook MK1](#rook-mk1) is a compact introduction to CoreXY.
- **Budget-friendly CoreXY:** [SimpleCore](#simplecore) offers triple-Z bed motion and an expandable frame for a first build.
- **Reuse existing printers:** [Duender](#duender) converts parts from two Ender-3 machines into a CoreXY.
- **Explore a different layout:** [Lemontron](#lemontron) is a portable inverted printer; check the revision notes before building.
- **Try a delta:** [Delta Flyer](#delta-flyer) pairs a small delta with a build manual.
- **Small, approachable build:** [Kappy](#kappy) is a beginner-oriented bedslinger with a creator-supplied manual and BOM.
- **Large format with IDEX:** [Rat Rig V-Core 4.1](#ratrig-v-core-4-1) offers 300–500 mm sizes, with Hybrid and IDEX upgrades; the kit is also available.

## Build from scratch

The primary sections group related XY motion layouts; the **CoreXY** section also includes VOSTOK’s Markforged-derived XY/2 belt-drive layout. “From scratch” includes projects that offer optional kits.

### CoreXY

Groups use a **representative printable X/Y size**: Large starts at 300×300 mm, Medium at 180×180 mm, and Small is below 180 mm on either axis. A scalable design can offer variants in other groups; its full range remains in the build-volume cell. Designs without a fixed or confirmed volume are placed by their intended format, with the uncertainty kept in that cell. CORE One and CORE One L have separate rows because they are distinct published models. Entries retain their previous relative order within each group.

### Mini footprint comparison

For a desk, **extra width and depth beyond printable XY** is easier to picture than an area ratio. Each value is exterior W minus printable X, and exterior D minus printable Y; it is not a required clearance on each side. The measurements use different body, enclosing, or open configurations, so check spool placement and bed travel before planning a tight space.

| Printer | Printable XY | Reported footprint | Extra W × D |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Positron V3.2](#positron-v3) (open) | 180×180 | 200×200 | +20×20 mm |
| [Cubiron](#cubiron) | 70×70 | 144×145 | +74×75 mm |
| [Quark](#quark) (approximate) | 24×24 | ~100×100 | ~+76×76 mm |
| [Encore](#encore) (body) | 120×120 | 219×221 | +99×101 mm |
| [Voron V0](#voron-v0) † | 120×120 | 230×230† | +110×110 mm† |
| [MiniCube (Kotvic)](#minicube-kotvic) (body) | 149×140 | 265×265 | +116×125 mm |
| [Tantillus](#tantillus) (printed frame) | 100×100 | 225×225 | +125×125 mm |
| [Ultimaker 2 Go](#ultimaker-2-go) (desktop) | 120×120 | 258×250 | +138×130 mm |
| [BabyCube](#babycube) (enclosing) | ~90×80 | ~248×272 | ~+158×192 mm |
| [Prusa MINI](#prusa-mini) (no spool or PSU) | 180×180 | 380×330 | +200×150 mm |

Kotvic's MiniCube grows to **265×340×350 mm** with the spool, extruder, and Bowden tube included. BabyCube's enclosing figure excludes its spool, Bowden tube, and wiring. Those accessory differences matter more than a small difference in the table's overhead.
